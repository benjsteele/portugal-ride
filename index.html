📝 Daily Updates

Day 6 — Faro 🏁

I won't deny it was tough. But I ran with the top group — Mike, Karen, Niall Leyden, Michael B and Aoife. Really enjoyed the challenge. The hills come at a time when your legs are pure cooked.

Best moment of the day: falling into a shop for ice creams and having just enough to cover them before a big 19% climb. The euphoria at 738 km was indescribable. Leydo and I chilled out and watched a biker group roll in. 🍦

What's Next 🔥

I've decided to train for the Mallorca 312 — a big step up, but I need the next fire under me.

A massive thank you to all the sponsors (I covered the costs of the trip myself). The stories and the impact on suicide were really powerful — the most important thing we can do is listen.

Top lessons learned:

  1. Always run based on heart rate.
  2. In 44°C heat, never turn down a water stop.
  3. Try and race to a diet plan.
  4. A towel burrito is amazing for drying clothes.
  5. Eat even when you're not hungry — by the time it catches you it's too late.
  6. Enjoy every day on the bike! 🚴

Hazy start to the day, rolling fields, lots of agriculture and a real feel for the rural middle. The people are friendly and the group are buzzing after tackling the heat wave.

⚠️ Three Mistakes to Avoid
  1. New foods on hard days — Do not try a strange pastry before a climb. Stick to gels and bananas on Days 1–3.
  2. Skipping post-ride fuel — Your next day starts the moment you stop. That chocolate milk matters.
  3. Over-eating fibre — Beans, lentils, and whole grains cause mid-ride digestive distress at high intensity.

💧 Precision Electrolyte Drink Strategy
Two-bottle rule — every day, no exceptions:
  • Bottle 1 (750 ml): 1 serving Precision mix = 60g carbs + 750mg sodium. Mix into 750 ml, not 500 ml — keeps carb concentration at ~8%, easier on the stomach at Zone 2 intensity.
  • Bottle 2 (750 ml): Plain water always. Top up at every café stop. On Days 4–6 add ¼ tsp table salt (free from café table) = ~500mg extra sodium.
DaysTemp / TerrainMix servingsSodium from mix/bottleExtra sodium action
Days 1–3Cooler · Douro Valley1/day750mgNone needed — cooler conditions, café food covers the rest
Days 4–6Alentejo heat · 28–32°C1–2/day750–1,500mgAdd ¼ tsp salt to plain water bottle — free at every café. Drink 500–750 ml/hour minimum.
Sodium maths (Days 4–6): At 97 kg in 30°C heat you'll lose ~900–1,200mg sodium/hour. Per hour you'll get: ~750mg from mix bottle + ~75mg from gels + ~500mg from ¼ tsp salt in water bottle = ~1,325mg/hr. Close enough — and café soup, tosta mista, and Coke add more. You don't need to buy anything else.

You have 4–6 spare servings of the mix — use a second bottle on Days 4 and 5 if you're sweating heavily or feel crampy. Coke at cafés (39g carbs + caffeine) is your best backup on any day.

Café stop rule: Always refill the plain water bottle. Do not water down the carb mix bottle — drink it down first, then refill fresh at the next stop.

🌡️ Heat Acclimation Protocol
Accumulate 5–6 heat exposures in the 8 days before departure (Fri May 15 → Sat May 23). Mix of passive sauna sessions and post-ride saunas — the slight core-temp elevation from a prior ride makes each sauna significantly more effective. Do not sauna on travel day (Sun May 24).

5–6 sauna sessions across 8 days — sufficient to trigger significant physiological adaptations.

🧠 Why This Works
  • Plasma volume expands significantly by day 5–7, improving your body's ability to keep blood flowing to skin and muscles simultaneously.
  • Sweating starts earlier and becomes more dilute — you cool better without losing as much sodium.
  • Heart rate and core temperature during exercise in the heat will be lower, making your Z2 target on the N2 easier to sustain across all 6 days.
  • Perceived exertion drops — huge on a 6-day tour through the Alentejo in late May.
💧 Hydration & Practical Tips
  • Weigh before and after each session (naked, dry towel). For every 1 kg lost, drink 1.5 litres with electrolytes over the next 2 hours. Don't skip the sodium.
  • Avoid alcohol on sauna days, or limit it heavily.
  • Cool down naturally after the sauna — a 10–15 min walk in fresh air, followed by a tepid shower. A walk on the beach is ideal.
  • Don't push through dizziness or nausea. Heat adaptation is about consistent, tolerable stress — not grit. Exit if you need to.
